Software case study · CILE · 2026

How 24 municipal procedures became one software workflow.

The field operation was repeatable. The paperwork was not. I designed and built two linked browser applications that reduced a road-opening permit workflow from 98 minutes to 11.

By Valentin Angenot 8-minute read Liège, Belgium
24 Municipal procedures translated into one operating path
98 → 11 Minutes required to prepare one permit package
€596,881 Projected workflow value over ten years

Internalising selected road-opening work made strategic sense for CILE. The bottleneck was not the excavation itself. It was the administrative process surrounding every intervention.

01 · The caseOne operation, 24 administrative realities.

CILE serves more than 560,000 residents across 24 municipalities. The same type of field intervention could require a different form, contact, deadline and submission route depending on the address: Powalco in one commune, the GODP police platform in Ans and Saint-Nicolas, EAGLE BE in Visé, plain e-mail elsewhere — each with its own signage-plan format, fees and validation circuit.

At roughly 480 interventions a year, that variation was more than an inconvenience. It created recurring administrative effort and made selected road-opening activities harder to bring in-house.

“The field work did not change at the municipal border. The administrative process did.” The design problem

02 · The solutionMove the complexity into the software.

I mapped the 24 local procedures, identified their common structure and converted the variation into a single data model. The product was then designed around one operator journey: enter the address and intervention once, receive the complete permit package.

The application geocodes the address, detects the competent authority, applies the relevant rules, populates the required documents, prepares the signage plan, assembles the recipients and tracks the submission.

Built in ten working days, it runs entirely in the browser and requires no server deployment or additional infrastructure. Claude accelerated implementation as a development partner; the procedure research, product logic, validation and financial model remained my responsibility.

03 · Under the hoodTwo applications. Zero servers.

The system is two single-page applications — a permit generator and a signage editor — that synchronise through the browser's local storage. The zero-server architecture is a deliberate choice, not a shortcut: the tool has to run on a CILE workstation or a field tablet with nothing to install, no backend to maintain, and no data leaving the machine.

Rules engine

24 municipalities as structured data

Each commune is a record: submission platform, notice periods, required signage format (Qualiroute, annotated capture, dimensioned plan), contacts, fees, validation circuit and urgency procedure.

Geocoding

Address to authority in one step

Structured Nominatim queries, debounced at 800 ms, with a postal-code fast path, tolerant commune matching and a supervised override for edge cases outside the base.

Document engine

Word files built from raw XML

The DOCX is assembled character by character in Office Open XML and zipped with JSZip — no document library — for pixel-level control over headers, tables, embedded plans and QR codes.

Detour computation

Deviations calculated, not drawn

The operator traces the blocked axis; three OSRM routing strategies run in parallel, candidates crossing the closure are excluded, and the best detour is rendered with arrows, metrics and a PNG export.

Validation

Four validators, live

Postal code against the CILE territory, date coherence with duration alerts, Belgian phone formats and working-hour checks — aggregated in real time to gate generation.

Field link

From plan to site manager

Placed signs are aggregated across map and plans into a quantified list, matched with a CRUD registry of site managers, and sent with the signage plans in one action.

04 · The details that matterWhere the engineering actually lives.

Rules live as data, not code.

Deadlines change. Contacts change. Fees change. Every municipal rule sits in external JSON files (communes.json, codes-postaux.json) loaded fresh at startup — so CILE updates a deadline by editing a file, never by shipping code.

Official municipal forms, filled automatically.

Where a commune imposes its own template, the generator loads the official Word file and injects the form values directly into its XML — preserving the municipal layout, checkboxes and letterhead while removing the manual copying.

E-mail attachments, despite the browser.

Browsers cannot attach files through mailto:. The workflow degrades gracefully through three strategies — native Web Share on mobile, clipboard-to-Gmail on desktop, plain mailto: as the universal fallback — so the package reaches the commune from any device.

The analytics were free.

Every generated permit records an 18-field entry locally, feeding a dashboard of volumes, urgency rates, duration percentiles and per-commune breakdowns — with CSV export ready for Excel. Operational reporting came as a by-product of the workflow itself.

05 · The resultFrom administrative friction to operating capacity.

Measured against the existing process, permit preparation fell from 98 minutes to 11. The gain was not only faster paperwork: a fragmented procedure became a repeatable operating capability.
